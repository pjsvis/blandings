# The repertory company — a casting sketch

Moira is our girl. The tuning loop (ear → probe → verdict → data) has,
over a week of listening, produced a casting doctrine as a by-product of
fixing mangles. This sketch persists it before it evaporates with the
session — what's ear-ruled, what's under probe, what's awaiting. It
graduates the day a production is greenlit (see below); until then it
is discussion, and cheap to keep.

## The company

- **Moira** — the house voice. Ear-tuned: lexicon, breaths, register
  doctrine, the house terms. Fully employed.
- **Moir** — the aunt. Register-cast: same tract, pitch base at the
  head. A *backup, conditional on the recital* — the aunt probe's long
  exhibit decides whether the register holds or slides into the husk.
  Currently in rehab (the cigarette lady's findings under control).
  Succeeds Moira at the Saramago Barrier — the fatigue drift, planned.
- **Daniel (en-GB), Fred (en-US)** — voice-cast candidates on the
  machine's roster. Unbooked. Unannounced arrivals get an audition,
  never a booking. Fred collides with Freddie by name; if cast, the
  ear names him.

## Two casting axes

- **Register-cast** (Moir): one tract, pitched. The succession physics
  *is* the pitch stickiness — the bug, twice a feature, now
  load-bearing: the handover costs nothing because it isn't a handover,
  it's the same voice aging on schedule with a name pinned to the end.
- **Voice-cast** (Daniel/Fred): a separate tract. No stickiness *by
  construction* — the cross-utterance reset problem Moir hasn't solved,
  solved by the operating system. Casting stays demand-led: Wodehouse's
  dialogue skews male, so the tenor has more airtime available than
  the aunt. Don't tell her.

## Audition doctrine

1. Unannounced arrivals get five minutes behind the curtain — raw,
   then in character. Precedent: the cigarette lady was an intruder in
   a probe, and she was Moir.
2. The ear names the winners. No voice enters the registry before the
   ear's signature — muppet-exclusion, applied to tracts.
3. Frequency is rent: airtime decides casting priority.

## The control case — Uncle Colm, never to be booked

Kevin McAleer's *Derry Girls* uncle: the monologue below the barrier,
held by inescapability where Saramago holds by craft — that line
between them *is* the barrier, made audible. Uniform density, the
house taxonomy's drone, no pauses where meaning demands them. Not a
voice talent for the long listen: the barrier's definition by
negative, and the pipeline's null hypothesis — register-castable by
omission (the flattener with every house instrument disabled, at a
rate drag). One audition no one attends; the ear rules by leaving
the room.

## What a production still lacks

The players and the script are awaited; so is the machinery between
script and sound. A radio-play pipeline needs:

- a **casting table** — speaker → voice + register, as data;
- a **director layer** — script → per-line say invocations (voice,
  rate, pitch, breaths);
- **assembly** — per-line renders concatenated; per-speaker processes
  give free resets;
- **direction as rate-matching** — schedule channel time for the
  listener's posterior: the pause after a question is the receiver's
  compute time. Shannon's excluded semantics, reintroduced at the
  physical layer, as a director's day job.

A skillful agent with radio-production skills could build this from
the sketch. The sketch is the brief for the agent we haven't hired.

## The gate

Adaptation and distribution of Wodehouse is rights-dependent,
jurisdiction-dependent, and unverified — Hume's Razor on our own
knowledge. The current regime is private dogfood reading of local
files, which the pipeline already does. The play does not clear the
gate until the production agent exists and the rights question is
asked and answered. The Derrida question comes before the eval, not
after.

## Graduation

The day production is greenlit, this sketch becomes a brief with a
callsign and a registry entry, and marks itself superseded here.
